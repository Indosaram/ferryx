## 3. Per-criterion inspection statement

### 3.1 Ownership — inspected, clean except F2-2 and F2-7

What I read and what it showed:

- **Identity-bound suspension ledger** (`terminal/suspension.rs`, `suspension/unix.rs`): a stop is
  claimed only after `observe` → `verify` → actuate → `observe` again, and the receipt is written
  only when the post-actuation identity equals the pre-actuation identity and `stopped == true`
  (`suspension.rs:99-137`). An already-stopped target is refused with `NotOwned` rather than
  claimed. `classify` revokes ownership on any mismatch and returns `External`; `resume` refuses
  anything that is not `FerryxOwned`. Linux pins with `pidfd_open` + `pidfd_send_signal` and
  validates the child relationship with `waitid(P_PIDFD, …, WNOWAIT)` before signalling; macOS
  re-verifies start time + `FERRYX_PTY_INCARNATION` immediately before and after `kill`, and the
  module comment states the residual PID-reuse window honestly. Unsigned/unverifiable platforms
  return `UnsupportedPlatform` instead of a receipt. **Clean** (F2-1 is about the Windows *feature*,
  not about the ledger's safety).
- **No pattern-based process matching anywhere in the product or the runner.** The runner's teardown
  signals only PIDs it spawned or descendants whose executable path was positively verified
  (`common-harness.mjs:617-641` `verifyProcessIdentity`, `:1180-1190` `if (descendant.identityVerified === false) continue;`), and explicitly documents the pass-5/pass-6 pattern-matching
  anti-pattern it exists to prevent. Unverified rows are reported, never signalled. **Clean.**
- **Teardown kills only what it started**: `reapProcess` escalates `SIGTERM`→`SIGKILL` on the
  task-owned process group and only then falls back to `child.kill()` on the single PID, with every
  step bounded by a deadline and the receipt re-derived from observation. **Clean.**
- **Handover ownership transfer**: the successor now refuses a session whose predecessor cannot
  prove an incarnation, whose declared workspace/worktree disagrees with the exported owner record,
  or that has no record at all (`server.rs:2858-2884` `adopt_transferred_ownership`); the record is
  installed *before* the PTY master is taken, and released again on every failure path
  (`:2898`, `:2910`, `:3026`, `:3053`). **Clean.**
- **No dual readers / no child kill on rollback**: `relinquish_transferred_session`
  (`pty.rs:1016-1031`) calls `begin_closing()` first, so the concurrently running lifecycle watcher
  sees `PtySessionState::Closing` and `continue`s (`pty.rs:579-582`) instead of reaching
  `close_session` → `SIGTERM`/`SIGKILL`; `close_authorized` also returns early on a failed
  `begin_closing` (`pty.rs:684-704`). The successor therefore releases its reader, writer and master
  fd without ever signalling the transferred child, which the predecessor resumes. **Clean** — this
  was the highest-risk path in the diff and it is correctly ordered.
- **Spawned-PTY incarnation**: minted once per spawn (`pty.rs:318`, `:395`), carried through
  `PtySessionSnapshot`, `PtySessionExport` and adoption, and cleared when it does not match the
  pid/incarnation pair it was minted for (`session.rs:264-266`, `:558-575`, `:1246-1247`).
  Paired proxies mint a per-actor incarnation (`paired_runtime.rs:337-348`) and remote sessions
  report the descriptor's own `client_request_id` as their incarnation rather than `None`. **Clean.**
- **The `SpawnOwnerGuard` gap**: F2-2. This is the one ownership invariant in the diff that is
  tested but not wired.
- **Bookkeeping leak**: F2-7.

the QA surface is compile-time gated out of the production package, no secret or token reaches an

3. **F2-10** — guard the new measurement against budget exhaustion, or charge it to no clock at all.

#### F2-10 — CLOSED

The finding was that the measurement was charged to the pre-trigger setup clock and
`MonotonicBudget.consume` **threw** when that clock was spent, so a measurement could become
`HarnessError('ASSERTION_FAILURE', 'monotonic budget exhausted …')` and hence verdict FAIL
(`classifyNativeFailure`, `pane-liveness.mjs:156-167`). Three independent guards now stand between
the measurement and that verdict, and each was read at the site:

- **The charge itself can no longer throw.** `resolveReadCharge` (`pane-binding.mjs:158-192`): on the
  no-`charge` path (the pane step's own budget) `budget.consume(...)` is called **inside a `try`**
  (`:162-163`) and a throw is converted to a refusal (`:164-172`); on the `charge` path the bound is
  `chargeBudget.remainingMs(capMs)` (`:177-179`) — `remainingMs`, **never `consume`**. That is the
  prior throw site `pane-binding.mjs:163-164` at `bcb76cfd`, and `common-harness.mjs:180-183`
  (`consume`'s `ASSERTION_FAILURE`) is byte-identical at both commits — the repair removed the
  *reachability*, not the class.
- **The read itself is typed.** `readInventory` is awaited inside `try` (`pane-binding.mjs:245-256`);
  a throw becomes `{ ok: false, code: SPLIT_INVENTORY_READ_FAILED, cause: error?.code, detail: … }`.
- **The refusal is typed, not thrown.** A bound ≤ 0 returns `INVENTORY_READ_REFUSED` with
  `cause: ATTEMPT_BUDGET_SPENT` (`pane-binding.mjs:181-190`, `:186`) and **no read is taken**.
- **The call site has a second guard.** `readSplitInventory` (`split-scenarios.mjs:132-156`) awaits
  `reader.snapshot(...)` inside `try` and, on a throw, records the typed action and returns `null`;
  the scenario continues. At `bcb76cfd` this function did not exist and `recordSplitInventory` ended
  in a bare `return reader.snapshot(...)` (`bcb76cfd:split-scenarios.mjs:137`).
- **No `consume` remains on a measurement path.** All 15 `consume(` sites in `split-scenarios.mjs`

  (`awaitReceipt`/`awaitReceiptForSession`/`awaitHeld`/`awaitMarkerReceiptForSession`), plus the

- **None of the four identities can be a verdict.** `SPLIT_INVENTORY_READ_FAILED`,
  `INVENTORY_READ_REFUSED`, `ATTEMPT_BUDGET_SPENT` and `APP_STDIO_BYTES_FAILED`

#### F2-11 — PARTIALLY CLOSED

read is only meaningful at the click instant), which is a defensible product argument but does not
close the finding. Closing it needs one of: raise `attemptCeilingMs` by the cap, or take the reads
outside the window.

  argument has to use.

  recorded, no: 2 s is only reached if the loopback read stalls to its cap. **For a borderline
  scenario, yes** — the ceiling check is unchanged and `remainingMs` only reads the clock, so
  instrumentation cost still counts in `attemptMs` and can turn a 14–15 s settlement into a

  (`INVENTORY_READ_REFUSED`, `cause: ATTEMPT_BUDGET_SPENT`) means the measurement **was not taken**:
  the split may or may not have created a session, and the evidence cannot answer the pass-21
  question at all. What it *can* no longer do is read as a negative, because the field values are
  unambiguous — on refusal or throw, `delta` is `null` and `sessionCount`/`sessionIds` are `null`,
  whereas a genuine zero delta has `ok: true`, `code: null` and `delta: { added: [], removed: [],
  beforeCount, afterCount }` with `beforeCount === afterCount`.
- **The exact field a future reader must check: `action.code` (with `action.cause`), read before
  `action.delta`.** The rule: `ok === true && code === null && delta !== null` ⇒ a measured delta;
  `code === 'INVENTORY_READ_REFUSED'` / `'SPLIT_INVENTORY_READ_FAILED'` / `'INVENTORY_READER_MISSING'`

- **The stage caps exclude it too.** `consume`/`remainingMs` read the same extended `deadlineAt`, so a

- **The exclusion is bounded**: at most 2 reads × `splitInventoryReadCapMs` (1 000 ms) ≈ 2 s, and each

#### The two nuances, disclosed

1. **The second read's `boundMs` is computed against the extended window.** Because the first read
   hands its wall clock back before the second is charged, a nearly exhausted window can make the
   second read payable where it would previously have been refused — by at most what the first read
   gave back. That is the intended semantic (the window is the scenario's own remaining budget) and it
   does not change what is measured: same moment, same `splitInventoryReadCapMs` cap, same charged and
   reported cost.
2. **`warmTargetMs` is now derived from `scenarioMs`, not from `attemptMs`.** The raw clock was the
   last place instrumentation could leak into a report. No test asserted it (`grep` found one
   producer, no consumer), so nothing was invalidated, but the reported `warmTargetMs` can now read
   `met` for a run whose wall clock exceeded 2 s.
